Source: Making of Modern Law; HathiTrust; Caselaw Access Project; the English Reports; citations read and linked by Claude.

Notes: The American editions of Henry John Stephen's Treatise on the Principles of Pleading in Civil Actions, placed by the year of the copy read, from Francis J. Troubat's editions of 1831 to 1857 through those of Franklin Fiske Heard (1867), Samuel Tyler (1871, 1882) and James DeWitt Andrews (1894 onward). One copy is counted for each year of printing: the official copy of the edition (a HathiTrust scan where there is one, else Gale's), or for a year with no official copy, such as the 1859 printing of the 1857 edition, the copy of that year read. Claude read every page of the copy, notes and appendix included, and looked each citation of a case up: a citation is American when it was found in the Caselaw Access Project or the New York Code Reporter, or is to an American reporter no source covers; English when it was found in the English Reports, or is to an English reporter they do not cover; and unknown when no case or reporter could be identified. Each citation printed counts, so a case cited twice counts twice. An edition marked * is only partly read, and its figures are of the pages read so far (a hollow point, an asterisk over the bar); editions not yet read are absent. The readings are a machine's and have not been checked by hand.
