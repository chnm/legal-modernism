Source: Caselaw Access Project; the English Reports; Legal Modernism citation detector and linker.

Notes: Each bar counts the citations that the opinions of the cases decided in one jurisdiction make to the reported cases of another, as the project's detector found them in the text of the opinions and its linker resolved them to a case in the Caselaw Access Project or the English Reports. A citation is one opinion citing one case: an opinion that cites a case under more than one string, as in a parallel citation, counts once. A jurisdiction is a state, a territory, the federal courts (U.S.) or the United Kingdom, the courts of England and Wales, Scotland and Ireland as the English Reports gather them. The bars are coloured by region, as the legend shows: New England, the Mid-Atlantic, the Midwest, the South, the Mountain West and the Pacific West, drawn from the Census Bureau's regions and divisions; the federal courts, the United Kingdom and the territories have colours of their own, and Dakota Territory is placed with the Dakotas. The years are those of the citing cases, in both directions, and run to 1920, where the opinions end. Citations to the jurisdiction's own cases are given in the subtitle and the table, not as a bar, and an opinion's citation of its own case is left out. The share of their citations is a bar's citations as a share of the other jurisdiction's own total in those years: the citations its cases make to all other jurisdictions, or the citations its cases receive from them, so that a jurisdiction with much law does not lead merely by having much law. A jurisdiction with fewer than 100 such citations in the years is not ranked by share; the table lists it. Citations the linker could resolve only to a stub case, or to the New York Code Reporter, are not counted. The counts are of linked citations: a case cited under a spelling or in a reporter the linker does not know yet is missing, and a few links are wrong.
