# Open questions from the consistency pass (issue #439)

These are passages where the editions of Stephen on Pleading differ, or where
the English Reports leave it unclear which record a cite belongs to, and no rule
in `README.md` settles it. For each, one interim reading has been applied to
every edition's records, and each changed citation's `note` ends "interim
reading pending the owners' decision (consistency pass)". Edition years below
are the copies read. "1831g", "1841g" and so on are Gale copies of the same
printing as the HathiTrust copy of that year. "1859g" is the 1859 printing of
the 1857 edition.

## 1. Yelv. 13: Wood v. Hawkshead or Crispe v. Viroll?

- **What the editions print:** all 24 records (every edition) print "Wood v.
  Hawkshead, Yelv. 13". The 1824 edition prints "Yelv. 13" with no name. The
  footnote supports a passage on a toll claim: the defendant pleads a statute
  that took back the liberties granted by Henry VI, and the plaintiff replies
  that 4 Hen. 7 revived them, which was held not to be a departure.
- **The English Reports:** Crispe v Viroll begins at Yel 13 (741 words). Wood v
  Haukshead begins at Yel 14 (767 words).
- **Interim reading:** Wood v. Haukshead, Yel 14 (17922886), because the name
  printed in 23 records and the subject (toll, departure) both point to it.
  `page` is 14 and `pincite` is unset. This was already the reading in every
  record.
- **Records changed:** 0.
- **Question:** Should a cite one page before the case the English Reports
  hold under its printed name be linked to that case at the corrected page, as
  now, or to the case that begins at the printed page?

## 2. "Per Finchden, 40 Ed. 3. 45" (1827 p. 293)

- **What the editions print:** 40 Ed. 3. 45 in 1824, 1827, 1835, 1838, 1841,
  1841g, 1843, 1845, 1851–59, 1867, 1894, 1895 and 1901. 49 Ed. 3. 45 in both
  1831 copies (hathi00002/02380, 19005816500/02300) and in Tyler's 1871 and
  1882 editions. The 49 is legible in all four scans, so it is a printed
  variant and not an OCR slip. The 1860 and 1866 editions drop the footnote.
- **Interim reading:** 49 is read as a misprint of 40 that began in the 1831
  American text and was copied into Tyler's editions. The four 49 records now
  have `volume` 40, at `medium`, with the printed 49 kept in `raw`. The 1824
  record was raised from `medium` to `high`, since most editions print 40. One
  point for the owners to check: Finchden is not known on the bench after
  about 1374 (48 Edw. III), which also argues against 49.
- **Records changed:** 5.
- **Question:** Is the 49 a misprint to correct to 40, as now, or a variant
  each edition's record should keep as printed?

## 3. "16 Hen. 6, 16" (1835 p. 418, footnote (h))

- **What the editions print:** 10 Hen. 6. 16 in 1824, 1827, both 1831 copies,
  Tyler's 1871 and 1882, and Andrews's 1894 and 1901. 16 Hen. 6, 16 in 1835 and
  the editions that follow its text: 1838, 1841, 1841g, 1843, 1845, 1851,
  1851g, 1854, 1854g, 1857, 1859g, 1867 and 1895. The 1860 and 1866 editions
  rewrite the footnote. Footnote (k) on the next page cites 10 Hen. 6. 16 in
  every edition (the garden case), for the same rule (a plea that amounts to
  the general issue).
- **Interim reading:** 16 is read as a misprint of 10, introduced in 1835. The
  14 records printing 16 now have `volume` 10, at `medium`, with the printed 16
  kept in `raw`. The 1871 and 1882 records, which print 10, went from `medium`
  to `high`. This goes against the count of editions (10 print 16, 7 print 10),
  because Stephen's own first editions and the next footnote both point to 10.
- **Records changed:** 16.
- **Question:** Is "16 Hen. 6, 16" a misprint of 10 Hen. 6. 16, as now, or a
  separate year book that the 1835 revision added on purpose?

## 4. "2 Saund. 291 c, n. (1)" against "201 c" (footnote (y), on an impossible or inconsistent date)

- **What the editions print:** 291 c, n. (1) in 1827, both 1831 copies, 1835,
  1838, 1843, 1871, 1882 and 1895. 201 c, n. (1) in the American editions of
  1841–67 (1841, 1841g, 1845, 1851, 1851g, 1854, 1854g, 1857, 1859g, 1867).
  Footnote (u) of the same passage cites 2 Saund. 291 n. (c) on the videlicet in
  every edition.
- **Records before:** the ten 201 records linked Goram v. Sweeting (2 Wms
  Saund 200, 18450278). One Gale copy's note says the owner identified Goram v.
  Sweeting there.
- **Interim reading:** 201 is read as a misprint of 291. The ten records now
  link Dakin's Case (2 Wms Saund 290, 18450055), with `page` 291, `pincite`,
  at `medium`. This follows the README's choice that 2 Saund. 291 n. (the
  videlicet) is Dakin's Case. A note numbered (1) also fits a case that begins
  a page earlier (Dakin's Case at 290) better than Goram v. Sweeting, whose
  notes there are numbered in the twenties.
- **Records changed:** 10.
- **Question:** Does the American editions' "2 Saund. 201 c, n. (1)" mean
  Williams's note at 291 c to Dakin's Case, as now, or Goram v. Sweeting, as
  first identified?

## 5. "2 Wms. Saund. 295, n. (3)" and "Carth. 2" (1838 p. 349, footnote (o), on replevin)

- **What the editions print:** 2 Saund. 285, n. (3) and Carth. 9 in 1824–41
  (and the American editions to 1867). 295 and Carth. 2 in 1838, 1843 and 1895.
  1866 prints 295 (hathi00008/03000) and 1854g prints 295 once
  (19005784900/02200). Footnote (n), just before, cites 2 Saund. 285, n. (3)
  in every edition.
- **Interim reading:** 295 is read as a misprint of 285: Williams's note (3)
  to Poole v. Longuevill (2 Wms Saund 282, 18450187), a replevin case.
  Carth. 2 is read as Carth. 9: Sanders v. Hussey (17410299), named in the
  same footnote. The 1838, 1843, 1854g and 1866 records already read it this
  way. The 1895 record (19005765700/03170, seq 3) linked White v. Stubbs at
  295 and was aligned. Its Carth. 2 already read 9.
- **Records changed:** 1.
- **Question:** Are 295 and Carth. 2 in the later English editions misprints
  of 285 and Carth. 9, as now?

## 6. Gwynne v. Burnell, 6 Bing. N.C. 463 (1860)

- **What the editions print:** 453, the first page of the case, in 1843,
  1866, 1867 and 1895 (where the OCR reads 458), and in 1860's third cite
  (19005903700/02330). 463 twice in 1860 (01170 seq 12, 01190 seq 7).
- **Interim reading:** 463 is kept as printed, as a pin cite. It falls inside
  Gwynne v. Burnell (6 Bing NC 453, 40,658 words). The README's rule is that a
  printed page inside the named case is kept as printed, even when another
  edition prints the first page.
- **Records changed:** 0.
- **Question:** Should 1860's 463 stay a pin cite, as now, or be read as a
  misprint of 453 like the other editions?

## 7. Vynior's Case, 8 Rep. 81 b and 82 b

- **The English Reports:** two records titled Vynior's Case: 8 Co Rep 80
  (15720465, 1,337 words, 77 E.R. 595) and 8 Co Rep 81 (15720466, 3,597 words,
  77 E.R. 597). Every edition cites 8 Rep. 81 b and 8 Rep. 82 b. All records
  link 15720466.
- **Records before:** 46 of the 81 b and 82 b records had `pincite: true`. Ten
  81 b records did not: both 1831 copies (two each), 1860 (two), 1866 (two)
  and 1895 (two).
- **Interim reading:** the two records are one case beginning at 80 (it is
  sometimes cited 8 Co. Rep. 80 a, 81 b), so 81 b and 82 b are pin cites. The
  ten records now have `pincite: true`.
- **Records changed:** 10.
- **Question:** Are the English Reports' records at 8 Co Rep 80 and 81 one
  case, so that 81 b is a pin cite, as now, or is 81 the case's first page?

## 8. Manser's Case, 2 Rep. 3

- **The English Reports:** "Painter v Manser" at 2 Co Rep 1 (15840012, no
  text, no `murrell_title`) and at 2 Co Rep 3 (15840013, 3,275 words, 76 E.R.
  392). The case is usually cited 2 Co. Rep. 3 a.
- **What the editions print:** Manser's Case, 2 Rep. 3 (24 records, every
  edition), 2 Rep. 4 and 2 Rep. 4 a (all linked 15840013, `pincite`).
- **Interim reading:** the empty record at 2 Co Rep 1 is not read as the
  case's beginning. The case begins at 3, so the 24 "2 Rep. 3" records no
  longer carry `pincite`. The "2 Rep. 4" and "4 a" records stay pin cites.
- **Records changed:** 24.
- **Question:** Should the empty English Reports record at 2 Co Rep 1 count
  as the start of Manser's Case (which would make 2 Rep. 3 a pin cite), or is
  3 its first page, as now?

## 9. Weedon v. Woodbridge, 13 Q.B. 470

- **The English Reports:** 13 QB 462 (18490107, 1849, 2,566 words) and 13 QB
  470 (18500179, 22 Jan. 1850, 4,344 words). The 1860, 1866, 1867 and 1895
  editions cite 470. All four records link 18500179.
- **Interim reading:** the two records are dated differently, so they are
  read as reports of separate stages of the suit. 470 is the first page of its
  own report, not a pin cite. The 1867 record (19005773800/01900, seq 10) was
  the only one marked `pincite` and was aligned.
- **Records changed:** 1.
- **Question:** Are the two Weedon v. Woodbridge records separate reports, as
  now, or one case beginning at 462?

## 11. Hawkins v. Eckles, 2 B. & P. 359, 361, n. (a)

- **What the editions print:** "Hawkins v. Eccles, 2 Bos. & Pul. 359, 361,
  n. (a)" in every edition (in 1894 the OCR reads 301).
- **Records before:** 1835, 1838, 1843, 1866 and 1895 recorded "361, n. (a)"
  as a second citation. The other 18 records (1824, 1827, both 1831 copies,
  1841, 1841g, 1845, 1851, 1851g, 1854, 1854g, 1857, 1859g, 1867, 1871, 1882,
  1894 and 1901) dropped it as a second number.
- **Interim reading:** the README rule that "a second page carrying its own
  note" is a second citation. In each of the 18 records the cite was split
  into two citations: 359, and 361 n. (a). The second is linked to the same
  case (18010122) as a pin cite, at `high` where a name is printed (`medium`
  in 1824). Later citations were renumbered.
- **Records changed:** 18 (18 citations added).
- **Question:** Is a later page of the same case with its own note a second
  citation, as the README now says, or should it be dropped like other second
  numbers?

## 12. "11 Rep. 25 b. 55 a" (Harpur's Case)

## 13. A second Saunders page after a note: "1 Saund. 276. n. (2). 211."

- **What the editions print:** 1824 (hathi00001/03980) prints "1 Saund. 276.
  n. (2). 211." with no note number on 211. Every later edition prints
  "211, n. (2)", and in 1894 and 1901 it is "id. 211". Note (b) on the same page
  cites 1 Saund. 211 alone, on the Statute of Frauds.
- **Records before:** every record had 211 as a second citation (Forth v.
  Stanton, 1 Wms Saund 210, 18450083), except the 1831 Gale copy
  (19005816500/03150), which had dropped "211 n. (2)" as a second number.
- **Interim reading:** 211 is a second citation in every edition. Under the
  README it is "a second page carrying its own note", and it lies in a
  different case from 276. The 1824 record keeps its second citation, since
  every other edition prints the note. The 1831 Gale record was split into two
  citations.
- **Records changed:** 1 (1 citation added).
- **Question:** When one edition prints a bare second page that other
  editions print with a note number, should that edition record it as a
  second citation, as now?

## 14. Dyer's volume

- **The English Reports:** Dyer in three volumes, `1 Dy`, `2 Dy` and `3 Dy`.
  The printed cites never show a volume.
- **Records before:** 275 linked Dyer citations already had the English
  Reports' volume (183 × 1, 44 × 2, 48 × 3). 63 had none: Sir Francis Leke's
  Case (3 Dy 365), Executors of Grenelife (1 Dy 42), Horneby v. Clifton
  (3 Dy 264), Cecil's Case (3 Dy 253) and Mynours v. Turke (1 Dy 65).
- **Interim reading:** `volume` is the English Reports' volume of the linked
  record. It was added to the 63, with a note that the printed cite does not
  show it, as for `2 Cowp`. No record had a volume that disagreed with its
  record. The one Dyer cite left `no_match` (1831g 19005816500/02140, "Dy.
  264 a") is now linked to Horneby v. Clifton (3 Dy 264) at `low`, as every
  other edition has it.
- **Records changed:** 63 citations in 55 files.
- **Question:** Should `volume` for Dyer, and the like, hold the English
  Reports' volume though the book is cited without one, as now?

## 18. "29 Ass. 37" against "29 Ap. xxxvii. 19 (E. 2)" (trial by inspection of nonage, through Viner)

- **What the editions print:** "29 Ass. 37" in 1824, 1827, both 1831 copies,
  Tyler's 1871 and 1882, and Andrews's 1901. Andrews's 1894 OCR reads "23 Ass.
  37" in badly garbled text. "29 Ap. xxxvii. 19 (E. 2)" in 1835, 1838, 1841,
  1841g, 1843, 1845, 1851–59, 1860, 1866, 1867 and 1895.
- **Records before:** "Ass." records at `high`. The "Ap." records split:
  `medium` and read as the Liber Assisarum in 1835, 1838, 1843, 1860 and 1866.
  `low` and "reign and folio uncertain" in the 11 others. 1894 had `volume` 23
  as a variant.
- **Interim reading:** all are the Liber Assisarum, 29 Edw. III, pl. 37
  (`volume` 29, no `page`, plea in `note`). "Ap. xxxvii." is read as a misprint
  of "Ass. 37" (xxxvii being 37). The 11 `low` "Ap." records went to `medium`.
  The 1894 record went to `volume` 29, its 23 read as OCR.
- **Records changed:** 12.
- **Question:** Is "29 Ap. xxxvii." the Liber Assisarum 29 Edw. III pl. 37
  misprinted, as now, and what is the "19 (E. 2)" after it?

## 19. Cites a page off from the English Reports record (other paging)

corrected it, and the printed page where not. This mixes the two conventions:

- **King v. Lyme Regis, Doug. 148** (record at 1 Doug 149, 17790084, the
  README's choice): `page` 148 in 22 records (1824–1895). `page` 149 in 1894
  (19005765000/04270) and 1901 (hathi00013/04660). `pincite: false` is set in

- **Price v. Brown, Str. 690** (record at 2 Str 691, 17952853): `page` 690 in
  1824, 1827 (two), both 1831 copies (two each), 1841 (two) and 1841g (two),

  1851–59, 1860, 1866, 1867, 1871, 1882, 1894, 1895, 1901).
- **Jeffrey v. White, Doug. 475** (record at 2 Doug 476, 17800101): `page`
  475 in 1827 and both 1831 copies. `page` 476 in the 16 others (1843 prints
  476).
- **Garrard v. Tuck, 8 C.B. 230** (record at 8 CB 231, 18490786): 1860 prints
  230 with `page` 231. 1866 prints 230 with `page` 230. 1867 prints 231.
- **Leke's Case, Dy. 364 b** (record at 3 Dy 365, 17941455): 1824
  (hathi00001/02840) prints "Dyer, 364. b." with `page` 364. 1901
  (hathi00013/03980) prints "364 b" with `page` 365. The other 43 records
  print 365, except 1894 19005765000/03620, whose OCR reads "301 b" (`page`

- **Records changed:** 0.
- **Question:** When a case is cited at another edition's paging a page from
